# Most-reported-offences-in-Canada
This project examines the most reported criminal offences in Canada using seven years of police-reported incident data. It focuses on ten frequently reported offence categories, exploring changes in their frequency, historical patterns, and rankings.
Through descriptive statistics and data visualization, the analysis transforms publicly available crime data into clear, meaningful insights.
Research Question: How have the frequency and rankings of Canada's most reported offences changed over the analysis period?
## Project Objectives
•	Identify the most frequently reported offence categories.
•	Analyze annual incident counts and historical patterns.
•	Calculate year-over-year and overall percentage changes.
•	Compare offence rankings over time.
•	Communicate findings through effective data visualization.

## Data Sources and Description
The project uses publicly available police-reported crime statistics.

##### Primary Source: Statistics Canada, Table 35-10-0177-01 — Incident-based crime statistics, by detailed violations.
##### Secondary Source: Crime Canada — Canada Crime Statistics: National Overview.
##### Supplementary Reference: Statistics Canada, Table 35-10-0026-01 — Crime Severity Index and weighted clearance rates.

## Scope
The project examines ten selected frequently reported offences, rather than all criminal offences in Canada.

##  Methodology
The analysis follows three stages:
Data Preparation: Review the dataset, validate numerical values, standardize offence categories, and organize observations chronologically.
Exploratory Analysis: Examine annual incident counts, calculate percentage changes, and compare offence rankings.
Data Visualization: Use static and animated charts to communicate historical patterns and changes in offence frequency.

##  Key Findings
The analysis identified five notable patterns:
Mischief remained the most reported offence. It recorded 307,909 incidents in the final year, representing an 8.4% increase from the beginning of the analysis period.
Uttering threats experienced the largest percentage increase. Reported incidents rose by approximately 20.4% over the analysis period.
Shoplifting recorded strong recent growth. Incidents increased by approximately 12.5% between the final two reporting years.
Breaking and entering experienced the largest percentage decline. Reported incidents decreased by approximately 19.7% over the analysis period.
Assault, Level 1, increased steadily after 2020. Reported incidents reached 219,951 in the final year, approximately 11.0% above the initial-year count.

Overall Finding: Seven categories recorded increases, while three declined. These differences demonstrate that offence categories followed distinct historical patterns.

## Limitations
The findings should be interpreted with the following considerations:
•	Selected offences: The dataset includes only ten offence categories.
•	Police-reported data: Not all criminal activity is reported to police.
•	Population changes: Incident counts are not adjusted for population growth.
•	Geographic coverage: National totals may conceal provincial and territorial differences.
•	Causation: Descriptive analysis cannot explain the underlying causes of observed changes.
Consequently, the findings do not represent a comprehensive assessment of overall crime levels in Canada.

## Conclusion
The analysis reveals distinct patterns among Canada's most reported criminal offences.
Mischief consistently recorded the highest incident counts, while uttering threats experienced the largest relative increase. Breaking and entering declined significantly, and shoplifting demonstrated notable recent growth.
These findings highlight the importance of examining individual offence categories rather than drawing broad conclusions from aggregated statistics.
The project demonstrates how descriptive analytics and effective visualization can transform publicly available data into meaningful, evidence-based insights.
